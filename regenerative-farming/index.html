Why we're doing this
Leaders in technology are not always leaders on nature. So we measure what is hardest to measure, on purpose.

Many of the people shaping the future live in New York, San Francisco and London. City life keeps most of us far from the soil that feeds us, and what we don't see, we don't value.

That's why BaseX makes it a mission: whenever impact is hard to quantify, we quantify it deliberately. The gap is rarely a lack of value. It's a lack of a mechanism to measure and price it. Regenerative farming is where we start.

Prices come from different schemes, years and methods, so they can't be added together. Where a price exists, it is usually the cost of the damage, not the value of the benefit. The EBF mapping is ours.

The counter-example

The old system sends two invoices.

The farmer pays the first: fertiliser, sprays and fuel. Everyone else pays the second, through water bills, lost soil and dirty air. It never appears on the farm's books.

£1.2B/yr

Cost of soil degradation in England and Wales. 80% of it falls off the farm.1

14.3%

Of England's surface waters reach good ecological status. Farm nutrients are one of the named pressures.16

61%

Of the phosphorus feeding Lough Neagh's algae comes from farming. The lough supplies 40%+ of Northern Ireland's drinking water.17,3

+31%

Modelled regenerative arable margin over conventional by year six, after a weaker start.18

The switch costs money up front: in the same model, food margin starts 64% below conventional before support payments18. A credible price on the benefits is what funds the bridge.

Valuable, but not yet priced

Wonder, reverence, connection with the divine.

These are real reasons people farm and protect land. The value is real. What's missing is a mechanism to measure and price it, and that is exactly the problem BaseX exists to solve.

Part of the mechanism already exists. The UK Treasury puts £13,000 on one point of life satisfaction, for one person, for one year9. The rest we build in the open, with the people who do the work.

Built on a shared language

EBF names six benefits. We go further.

The Ecological Benefits Framework, a program of The Lexicon, describes impact across six domains: Air, Water, Soil, Biodiversity, Equity and Carbon22. BaseX already uses these as claim tags, so regenerative claims stay comparable.

Seven of the 16 benefits fall outside those six, including welfare, nutrition, wellbeing and spiritual connection. We price those too.
